# Design Method

Also read `instructional-playbook.md` in this folder: taste rules distilled from real enablement programs. Playbook rules win ties.

## 0. Design for a short attention span

Assume the learner gives you 30 seconds before deciding to stay, and 5-10 minutes total. Rules:

- **Hook in the first 30 seconds** with something that costs them money or credibility today ("a rep lost a deal last week because..."), never with an agenda or definitions.
- **5-10 minute units.** Anything longer gets split into drop-in modules, each with a standalone outcome. A learner who leaves after any unit still walks away with something usable.
- **Interaction every ~90 seconds.** A click, a guess, a prediction, a poll. If the learner hasn't done anything in 2 minutes, redesign.
- **Chunk to 3.** Three bullets, three examples, three issues per block. Top-3 from real signal, never exhaustive.
- **TL;DR first, detail on demand.** The full answer is one screen; everything else is disclosure.
- **Make them guess before you tell.** A prediction ("what % of calls touch payments?") buys 2 minutes of attention because they now want to know if they were right.

## 0.5 General to specific: the zoom path

Sequence information like a camera zooming in. Each level answers the question the learner has *right now*, and creates the next question:

1. **Why this matters to YOU** (relevance hook, 1-2 sentences). Creates: "ok, what is it?"
2. **The whole thing in one sentence or one picture** (the mental model / advance organizer). Creates: "what are the parts?"
3. **The map**: parts named, relationships shown, nothing explained yet. Creates: "how does part 1 work?"
4. **One concrete running example** enters (Maria, Gymshark) and walks the map. Creates: "what about my situation?"
5. **Zoom into one part at a time**, example first, term second. Never open two zoom levels at once.
6. **Edge cases and exceptions last.** They only make sense once the normal case is solid.
7. **Back out to the map**, then apply: the learner places their own deal/scenario on the map.

The test at each level: could the learner stop here and still hold a correct (if incomplete) model? If a level breaks that, it's ordered wrong.

## 1. Needs Analysis (always first)

Ask before designing. Minimum viable questions:
1. What should the audience DO differently after this? (behavior, not knowledge)
2. Who exactly is the audience, and what do they already know/do today?
3. Why now? What deal, launch, or gap triggered this?
4. What does success look like in 30/60/90 days, and how would we see it?
5. What constraints? (time, format, live vs async, exec sponsor, deadline)

If the user can't answer #1, stop and workshop it. A program without a target behavior is a content dump.

## 2. Backward Design Chain

Design in this order, never the reverse:

```
Business outcome -> Behavior change -> Skills/knowledge needed -> Practice activities -> Content -> Reinforcement
```

Content comes second to last. Most bad training starts with content.

## 2.5 Adult learning methodology (andragogy, applied)

Adults learn differently. Design against each principle concretely:

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Need to know why** | Open with the field problem this solves, tied to their deals, quota, or credibility. Never "by the end of this session you will..." |
| **They bring experience** | Start by drawing theirs out: blank-slate observation windows, "what have you seen on calls?", peer exchange. Their war stories are content. |
| **Self-direction** | Give choice: pick a track, choose the scenario, self-serve paths, "I'm feeling lucky." Mandated linear courses get resented. |
| **Problem-centered, not subject-centered** | Organize around situations they face ("the CFO asks why your rate is higher") not topics ("Module 4: Pricing"). |
| **Immediate applicability** | They must use it this week or it evaporates. End every unit with: "what will you do with this on your next call?" and a grab-mid-deal artifact. |
| **Respect** | Never quiz on trivia, never make an expert feel remedial. Calibrate entry points; let people test out. |

Relevance is engineered, not asserted: real merchant names they recognize, real objections from call transcripts, numbers at their deal sizes, their region's rules. If an example could appear in any company's training, it's not relevant enough.

## 3. Session Design Defaults (live enablement)

- Max 20% lecture. The rest: demo, practice, discussion, application to real deals.
- Open with the "why now" tied to their pipeline, not agenda slides.
- Every session ends with a commitment: "what will you do with this in your next call?"
- Gagne's events as a sanity check: attention -> objective -> recall prior -> present -> guide -> practice -> feedback -> assess -> transfer.

## 4. Reinforcement (non-negotiable)

The forgetting curve wins without spacing. Every live session gets:
- +1 week: quick application prompt or manager talking point
- +1 month: check-in, peer share, or short scenario exercise
- Artifact they can grab mid-deal (one-pager, talk track, checklist)

## 5. Measurement

Map to Kirkpatrick but lead with behavior (level 3):

| Level | What | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Reaction | Did they find it useful? | Post-session pulse, 1 question |
| 2 Learning | Can they do it in the room? | Practice output, scenario score |
| 3 Behavior | Are they doing it in the field? | Call reviews, Gong snippets, manager spot checks |
| 4 Results | Did the number move? | Win rate, deal size, ramp time (directional, be honest about attribution) |

Phrase level 3 as a real question, e.g. "are SEs carrying more of the retail conversation."
In-session metrics stay out of Vault project descriptions (Fellow/notes, surfaced in progress updates).

## 6. Content Critique Checklist

When reviewing a deck or asset, score against:
- [ ] Clear target behavior stated up front
- [ ] Opens with audience relevance, not agenda
- [ ] Lecture-to-practice ratio (flag if >30% telling)
- [ ] Examples are from the audience's world (real deal types, real objections)
- [ ] Cognitive load: one idea per slide/section, no wall-of-text slides
- [ ] Bloom alignment: activities match the verb (if objective says "apply", there must be application)
- [ ] Reinforcement plan exists
- [ ] Measurement is behavior-based, not completion-based

Present findings as a table: issue / why it matters / suggested fix.
